2. What StreetDrill does

StreetDrill is a study app. Its games teach the streets, addresses, fire station districts and target hazards of the city you work in, using public map data that the app downloads once and then uses offline. Cities already on the list download right away; other US cities are built on request. Routes in the games come from a model of the street network built for study.

StreetDrill does not dispatch calls, give live turn-by-turn directions, know about closures or traffic, or tell you how to respond to an incident.

3. A study aid, not a dispatch tool: please read this

StreetDrill is not a City or fire department app. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or acting for any city, fire department or other agency. The data sources page lists where its map data comes from.

That data and the routes built from it can be wrong, incomplete or out of date:

  • Streets and addresses change, and some newer streets and addresses aren't in the data yet. Fire hydrants aren't included.
  • Routes are a study model. They don't know about closures, construction, traffic, apparatus limits or your department's preferred routes.
  • Station details, districts and target hazards are compiled from public sources and may not match your department's current information. Outside Richardson, first-due areas are estimated by drive time, and in cities built on request, stations are estimates drafted from OpenStreetMap.

So please:

  • Use StreetDrill to study, not to respond. On a call, use your department's maps, CAD and MDT, dispatch, and your officer's direction.
  • Never use StreetDrill while driving, on duty or off. Pull over first, and follow the law and your department's rules on phone use.
  • Follow your department's SOGs, and check routes and facts against official sources before relying on them, including for a promotional exam.
