Privacy Policy

Last updated November 4, 2026

StreetDrill is a street study app that runs on your phone, with no tracking. This page explains what the app keeps on your phone, the little that leaves it (such as downloading your city, buying the unlock, or checking a sign-in for free access), how this website counts visits, and how to delete it all.

Who we are

StreetDrill is made by Hantis USA, LLC ("we", "us"). This policy covers the StreetDrill app for iPhone, iPad and Android, including test builds, and this website, streetdrill.app.

What stays on your phone

StreetDrill saves these on your phone so it can work offline. They aren't sent to us or anyone else.

Your sign-in

  • The name you enter, or the id, name and email of the Apple or Google account you continue with, and what you're studying for (for example, driver promotion). For beta testers, the Tester ID; access codes are checked on the phone. There's no account server: these only tell people apart on that phone, and each person's progress is kept separately.

Your settings

  • Your city and the cities you've downloaded, your home base (a fire station or an address you choose), study area, difficulty, and voice, sound, haptics and appearance choices.

Your progress

  • XP, level and streak, stats for each game, how well you know each street, hazard and block, your session history and route book, and the ratings and notes you give each game, kept separately for each city.

What we don't collect

  • StreetDrill uses your phone's location only when you ask it to, and never keeps it. Use my location in city setup finds the city you're in: your phone's own map service (Apple's or Google's) turns the position into a place name, and StreetDrill keeps only the city. Glance shows the street you're on while it's open, worked out on the phone; nothing is sent anywhere.
  • It doesn't access your contacts, photos, camera or microphone. The dispatcher voice is made on your phone.
  • The app contains no advertising and no analytics or tracking tools.
  • We never sell your information or share it for advertising.

What leaves your phone

Your city

The app downloads your city's map from our server at api.streetdrill.app. If you ask for a city that isn't built yet, it sends the city's name and state to our city builder at api.streetdrill.app, which builds the city on GitHub and adds it to the list for everyone, with nothing about who asked. To limit requests to three new cities per internet connection a day, the city builder keeps a count for each IP address for two days.

Signing in with Apple or Google

Apple or Google confirms your account and gives the app its id, name and email, which stay on your phone.

Some accounts get StreetDrill free: beta testers, and the first people to sign in. To check yours, the app sends the sign-in's signed token, which Apple or Google issued for StreetDrill, to our server at api.streetdrill.app, together with the app's anonymous purchase ID (see Buying StreetDrill). The server checks the token with Apple or Google and compares its email with our tester list. It keeps your account's id only if that account gets free access, so the same account keeps it on any phone. It doesn't keep your name or email.

Buying StreetDrill

The one-time purchase that unlocks StreetDrill goes through the App Store or Google Play. We never see your card or payment details. RevenueCat, which checks purchases for us, receives the purchase record from Apple or Google and an anonymous ID the app makes for this install, so it can confirm the purchase and bring it back with Restore purchases on another phone. If your account gets free access, our server records that with RevenueCat under the same anonymous ID.

Your progress report, when you send it

In Progress, Share progress report opens your phone's share sheet with a report you can read first: your name, Tester ID and role, your rating and notes for each game, sessions, accuracy and weakest streets, and the questions you flagged. It goes only where you send it, usually a text or email to us. We use it to improve StreetDrill.

Map problem reports, when you send one

Report a map problem (in Profile, or the flag on a station or street on the map) sends what you pick and write, such as the station or street and the right number or address, to our server at api.streetdrill.app, which emails it to us at support@streetdrill.app. With it go your city, department and study area, your home base if it's a fire station, the date of the map data, and the app version and type of phone, so we can find the problem in the map you saw. It doesn't include your name, email or location. To keep junk out, the server counts the reports from each internet connection for two days, under a one-way code made from the IP address rather than the address itself. If the app can't reach the server, it opens the report in your mail app instead, and you send it yourself. We use reports only to fix the map.

Backups, when you make one

Back up progress shares your progress as text, wherever you choose to send it, so you can restore it on another phone.

Emails you send us

If you email us, for tester access or support, we receive your address and whatever you write, and use them to reply and to set up your tester access.

App updates

StreetDrill can download updates from Expo's update service. Like any download, that request includes your phone's IP address and technical details the update check needs, such as the app version, the platform and an anonymous install ID. It never includes your progress or anything you type.

This website

streetdrill.app uses Google Analytics to count visits and see what's working. It records the pages you view, how you got here (a search or a link, for example), your browser and type of device, your approximate location (the city, worked out from your IP address, which Google Analytics doesn't keep) and taps on the tester-access and "my city" email links.

To tell visits apart without knowing who you are, it sets Google Analytics cookies (_ga and _ga_ followed by an id) and keeps a random visitor id (lytics_vid) in your browser's storage. None of it is used for advertising. Google explains how it uses information from sites that use its services.

To browse without it, block cookies or scripts from Google in your browser or with a content blocker, or use Google's opt-out browser add-on. Like any website, our web host (GitHub) also sees your IP address when you visit.

How long we keep it

  • What's on your phone stays there until you reset your progress or delete the app.
  • Progress reports and emails you send us are kept while StreetDrill is in testing, then deleted when we no longer need them. You can ask us to delete yours at any time.
  • Website visit data is kept in Google Analytics for up to 14 months.
  • Map problem reports are kept until the problem is fixed, then deleted when we no longer need them.
  • The server's counts of city requests and map problem reports per internet connection expire after two days.
  • The account ids our server keeps for free access are kept while StreetDrill offers it, so the same account keeps free access. Purchase records are kept by Apple, Google and RevenueCat as their own policies describe.

Delete your data

On your phone:

  1. Open Progress and tap Reset progress to clear XP, your streak, game stats, mastery, history and ratings.
  2. Or delete the StreetDrill app, which removes everything it saved.

Signing out in Profile keeps your progress on the phone until a different Tester ID signs in there.

Free access and purchase records: email support@streetdrill.app and we'll delete the account id our server keeps and the records RevenueCat holds for you. Deleting the free-access record ends free access for that account. A purchase stays with your App Store or Google Play account either way.

Reports and emails you sent us: email support@streetdrill.app with the subject "Delete my data". We'll confirm and delete them within 30 days.

Your choices

  • You decide whether to send a progress report, and you can read it before you do.
  • You can browse streetdrill.app without Google Analytics, as This website explains.
  • You can ask us for a copy of what we hold about you, or to correct or delete it, at support@streetdrill.app. Depending on where you live, you may have further rights under local law; we'll honor them.

Children

StreetDrill is meant for firefighters and isn't directed at children under 13. We don't knowingly collect information from them. If you think a child has sent us information, contact us and we'll delete it.

Where your data is processed

StreetDrill is run from the United States. What you send us is processed in the United States and wherever our providers operate.

Changes to this policy

If we change this policy, we'll update the date at the top. For significant changes, such as the app starting to send anything new off your phone, we'll tell you in the app before they take effect.
