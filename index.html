Your misses come back

Response Run

Run real calls from quarters

Tap Generate Random Call and a real Richardson address comes in like a dispatch. Then make every move: which way out of the bay, every turn, and which side the address is on.

  • Real addresses. About 31,700 official address points, in your first due or anywhere in the city.
  • Good alternates count. A near-equal route is right, the way it is on a real run.
  • Hospital and target hazard runs. Practice the trips you can't afford to hunt for.

Dispatch Drill

Hear the call. Find it fast.

Alert tones and a dispatcher voice read the call type, address and cross streets. Drop a pin on a blank map, then make your first move out of the station.

  • Built for the radio. Turn an address you hear into a spot on the map.
  • On the clock. Your time starts when the dispatcher stops talking.
  • Replay any time. Or read the call as text.

Every street

Know the map without the names

A street lights up on a blank map and you name it. Then hundred blocks, cross streets, and the order you reach them along every main road.

  • Blank maps, like the test. No street names on Standard and Expert.
  • Hundred blocks. Read any address number as a place on the map.
  • Cross streets. What crosses what, and what comes next on every corridor.

Test prep

Walk into the map test ready

The Exam Simulator runs a timed mock driver/engineer map test and scores it by category. Progress shows what you know, and what still needs work.

  • Exam Simulator. Routes from quarters, hundred blocks, cross streets, target hazards and first-due areas, with a pass/fail report.
  • Shift Drill. Ten mixed questions a day that lean on your weak spots. Days off on a 48/96 don't break your streak.
  • Review Misses. Re-drill only what you got wrong.

Questions

Good to know

Where does the map data come from?

From City of Richardson open data (address points, official street names, city limits and fire station districts) and OpenStreetMap (street shapes, one-ways and signals). See every source, with a link to each one.

Is StreetDrill made by the City or the fire department?

No. StreetDrill is an independent study aid made by Hantis USA, LLC. It isn't affiliated with or endorsed by the City of Richardson or Richardson Fire Department. Always follow your department's SOGs, and verify routes before relying on them.

Does it need a signal?

No. The streets, addresses, stations and target hazards all ship inside the app, so every game works offline.

Does StreetDrill track my location?

No. It never uses your location, and your progress stays on your phone. No ads, no tracking. Read the privacy policy.

What does it cost?

Nothing while it's in testing. Testers get every game.

How do I get it?

StreetDrill is in testing on iPhone. Request tester access and we'll send your Tester ID, access code and install steps.

Is it on Android?

Not yet. StreetDrill is iPhone only for now.
